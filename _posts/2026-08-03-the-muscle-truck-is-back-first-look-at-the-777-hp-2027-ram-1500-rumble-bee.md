# The Muscle Truck Is Back: A First Look at the 2027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee

## Mopar Monday

The muscle truck is officially back—and Ram did not return quietly.

The 2027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee is far more than a regular pickup with special graphics and a familiar badge. Ram has developed an entire family of street-focused performance trucks with HEMI V8 power, a shortened wheelbase, widebody styling and hardware engineered for acceleration, cornering and high-speed driving.

I will be honest: this is one of the most exciting vehicles Ram has introduced in years.

The lineup will include four models: the Rumble Bee, Rumble Bee 392, Rumble Bee 392 Track Pack and range-topping Rumble Bee SRT. Buyers will have three HEMI V8 engines to choose from, producing between 395 and an incredible 777 horsepower.

This is not another off-road truck competing with the Ram TRX. The Rumble Bee was created for pavement, drag strips and racetracks.

## Three Levels of HEMI Power

The entry-level Rumble Bee receives the familiar 5.7-liter HEMI V8, producing 395 horsepower and 410 pound-feet of torque.

Ram estimates that it will accelerate from zero to 60 mph in 6.1 seconds and complete the quarter-mile in 14.6 seconds at 93 mph. Those numbers will not challenge the SRT, but they are still respectable for a full-size pickup—and the sound and character of a naturally aspirated HEMI should be a major part of the experience.

Moving up to the Rumble Bee 392 introduces a 6.4-liter HEMI V8 producing 470 horsepower and 455 pound-feet of torque. This is the first factory application of the 392-cubic-inch HEMI in the Ram 1500 platform.

Ram lists a zero-to-60-mph time of 5.2 seconds and a quarter-mile time of 13.2 seconds at 101 mph.

The Rumble Bee 392 Track Pack uses the same 470-horsepower engine but adds serious performance equipment, including air suspension, semi-active shocks, larger brakes, wider tires, Track Mode, Launch Control and an electronic spool differential.

Then there is the Rumble Bee SRT.

Its supercharged 6.2-liter HEMI V8 produces 777 horsepower and 680 pound-feet of torque. Ram says the SRT will reach 60 mph in 3.4 seconds, complete the quarter-mile in 11.6 seconds at 116 mph and achieve a targeted top speed of 170 mph.

Ram describes it as the most powerful, quickest and fastest V8-powered production pickup ever built. Those are extraordinary numbers for any vehicle, let alone a pickup that can still tow a trailer.

## Built for the Street, Not the Trail

The Rumble Bee and Ram TRX may share a manufacturer and plenty of horsepower, but their missions are completely different.

The TRX is designed to travel quickly across rough terrain. The Rumble Bee sits lower, wears street-performance tires and uses aerodynamic components intended to keep it stable at speed.

Every Rumble Bee will be offered exclusively as a Quad Cab with a short bed. Ram removed 13 inches from the frame between the axles, reducing frame flex by 10 percent and creating a shorter, more responsive truck.

The finished truck measures 219.5 inches long and approximately 88 inches wide. Its appearance is defined by widebody fenders, unique sheet metal, a performance front fascia and model-specific aerodynamic components.

The Rumble Bee and Rumble Bee 392 use steel coil springs with performance-tuned Bilstein monotube shocks. They also receive wider front suspension arms, a wider rear axle and larger stabilizer bars to reduce body roll.

The 392 Track Pack and SRT receive standard four-corner air suspension with Bilstein DAMPTRONIC SKY semi-active shocks. In Track Mode, the suspension can lower the truck by 1.5 inches to improve handling and reduce its center of gravity.

Those upper-performance models also receive 16.1-inch front brake rotors with six-piston Brembo calipers, a dedicated Track Mode and Launch Control.

## Some of the Widest Tires Ram Has Ever Used

The Rumble Bee and Rumble Bee 392 ride on 285/45R22 all-season performance tires mounted on 22-by-10-inch wheels.

The 392 Track Pack and SRT step up to massive 325/40R22 Goodyear all-season tires mounted on 22-by-12-inch wheels. Ram says this is the widest production-vehicle wheel-and-tire combination the company has offered since the Dodge Viper.

That wide stance is not only for appearance. Ram claims the Track Pack and SRT can generate 0.89 g of lateral grip, representing an improvement of more than 20 percent over the entry-level Rumble Bee.

Functional aerodynamic components—including a deep front splitter, underbody aero shield and substantial tailgate spoiler—are designed to generate 192 pounds of downforce at 170 mph.

This is genuine performance engineering, not simply aggressive styling.

## Four-Wheel-Drive Traction With a Rear-Wheel-Drive Button

Every Rumble Bee uses full-time active four-wheel drive with variable torque distribution.

However, Ram has not forgotten what muscle-truck buyers want. A dedicated RWD button disconnects the front axle, allowing every Rumble Bee to operate in rear-wheel drive.

The 392 Track Pack and SRT add an electronic spool differential that can lock the rear axle and distribute power evenly between the rear tires. Combined with Launch Control and 325-millimeter tires, that should make these trucks serious performers at the drag strip.

It should also make producing tire smoke remarkably easy.

## It Is Still a Pickup Truck

Despite its street-performance focus, the Rumble Bee has not completely abandoned traditional truck capability.

Ram lists maximum towing capacity of up to 8,890 pounds and maximum payload capacity of up to 1,160 pounds, depending on the model and configuration.

That should make the Rumble Bee usable as a daily driver and occasional tow vehicle. However, the exclusive Quad Cab and short-bed layout will provide less rear-seat and cargo space than a conventional Crew Cab Ram 1500.

This will not be the best choice for every truck buyer. That is also part of what makes it interesting. The Rumble Bee is being built around a specific purpose rather than attempting to satisfy everyone.

## A Driver-Focused Interior

Every Rumble Bee receives a 12.3-inch digital instrument cluster, flat-bottom performance steering wheel, aluminum paddle shifters and a console-mounted G/T shifter.

The entry-level truck receives an 8.4-inch Uconnect touchscreen, while the Rumble Bee 392 upgrades to a 12-inch display and a 10-speaker Alpine audio system.

The Rumble Bee SRT tops the range with a 14.5-inch portrait-style touchscreen, 19-speaker Harman Kardon audio system, 12-way power-adjustable front seats and leather-and-suede upholstery. Carbon-fiber trim, Desert Orange stitching and a suede headliner help separate it from the rest of the lineup.

The 392 Track Pack also receives heavily bolstered leather-and-suede seats, Prowler Yellow stitching and model-specific interior details.

Ram has given each version its own personality instead of reserving every desirable feature for the most expensive model.

## Our Initial Take

The 2027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee may be one of the most interesting trucks Ram has introduced in decades.

The 777-horsepower SRT will understandably receive most of the attention, but I am nearly as interested in the two naturally aspirated models.

The entry-level 5.7-liter Rumble Bee could become the most important member of the lineup. It offers the shortened chassis, widebody appearance, HEMI V8 sound and rear-wheel-drive mode without forcing buyers to immediately step up to the supercharged SRT.

The 392 may be the sweet spot. Its naturally aspirated 6.4-liter HEMI produces 470 horsepower and could offer an excellent combination of performance, sound and everyday usability.

Personally, the 392 is the version I am most eager to experience. It should provide considerably more performance than the 5.7-liter without the likely price, fuel consumption and insurance costs of the 777-horsepower SRT.

That could change once Ram announces pricing.

## The Biggest Unanswered Question

Ram has not announced official U.S. pricing for any Rumble Bee model, and the trucks currently being displayed are preproduction vehicles. Final equipment, features and availability could change before production begins.

Price will determine whether the Rumble Bee becomes an attainable performance truck or another exciting machine that remains beyond the reach of most enthusiasts.

Ram says the 5.7-liter Rumble Bee is expected to become available beginning in late 2026. The 392 and SRT versions are scheduled to arrive during the first half of 2027, with exact availability varying by model.

For now, the Rumble Bee appears to be exactly what its name suggests: loud, aggressive, slightly impractical and built to sting.

I am genuinely excited to see this truck reach production—and even more excited about the possibility of eventually getting behind the wheel.
